## Intro

Not two roles. One loop.

I found what breaks before I learned to build. It changed how I build
everything since — I never got the illusion that finished and safe are the
same word.

&nbsp;

## How it started

I wrote my first port scanner because I couldn't afford one. Not for a
course, not for a job — it just bothered me that my own laptop could be
staring at the internet with every door unlocked and nobody would tell me.
Scanning my own machine for hours, reading whatever it found. That's when
this stopped being a major and became a habit.

&nbsp;

## What I did with it

I triaged 200+ alerts a day for a global drug manufacturer and learned that
security is mostly people: whoever is awake at 2am, how fast they trust a
chart, whether anyone reads the escalation. I built a vulnerability
researcher's habit of asking the only question that matters — does it
*actually* affect you, or can it be safely ignored — and then wrote the tool
that answers it, because nobody else would.

Then I crossed the line I was told exists between the two sides. I founded
Buildora and kept ~50 clients alive on sites I ship myself. I co-founded
Crypton because passwords are the weakest link in every stack I had ever
probed, and passkeys bound to actual devices were how I wanted to end
that. I built the combat-sports platform so each stakeholder finally sits in
one place. Local OCR that reads invoices. An extension that rewrites
prompts across three AI chats. A voice made of blinks, for ICU patients.

&nbsp;

## The loop

Break things in the lab. Build things in the field. Notice what the builder
in you never looks for, and what the breaker in you never protects.

That loop is the whole trick. It is also, honestly, the reason I don't
sleep enough — but every project on this site is a trip around it.
